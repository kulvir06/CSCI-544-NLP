Two checks catch what either one misses.

We tested an unverified RAG control, temporal verification, meaning verification and the combined safety gate on 24 reviewed F-1 employment cases. The combined verifier made the expected decision in 22 cases and blocked 16 of 17 unsafe explanations.

Qualitative evidence

Why the two verifiers are complementary

One example is faithful to the wrong rule. The other uses the right rule but reverses its meaning.

Temporal failure · TEST-05

How long was STEM OPT in 2020?

“This interim rule will allow ... an extension ... for up to 17 months.”

Meaning verifierPasses: “17 months” matches the source
Temporal verifierBlocks: the claim was superseded in 2016

Meaning failure · TEST-10

May a student begin OPT before the approved date?

“A student may not begin employment before the approved start date.”

Temporal verifierPasses: the source applies in 2026
Meaning verifierBlocks: removing “not” reverses the rule

Final takeaway Safe legal explanations require the right rule and preserved meaning.

The current evaluation covers F-1 employment and extension rules. Expanding to other legal domains requires authoritative documents, reviewed benchmarks and temporal re-indexing whenever regulations change.
